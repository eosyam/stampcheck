Why your email button looks flat in Outlook (and the bulletproof fix)

By Eray, who builds Stampcheck · Published 4 November 2026

Short answer: classic Outlook ignores border-radius and padding on links, so a styled <a> loses its shape. Use a "bulletproof" button: a VML rounded rectangle that only Outlook sees, and a normal styled link for every other client.

The bulletproof button

Copy this and change the link, colors, size and label. Keep the width and height in both parts the same.

<!--[if mso]>
<v:roundrect xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml" xmlns:w="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:office:word"
  href="https://example.com" style="height:44px;v-text-anchor:middle;width:200px;"
  arcsize="12%" stroke="f" fillcolor="#1D44B8">
  <w:anchorlock/>
  <center style="color:#ffffff;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;font-size:16px;font-weight:bold;">Get started</center>
</v:roundrect>
<![endif]-->
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<a href="https://example.com" style="background:#1D44B8;border-radius:5px;color:#ffffff;display:inline-block;
  font-family:Arial,sans-serif;font-size:16px;font-weight:bold;line-height:44px;text-align:center;
  text-decoration:none;width:200px;">Get started</a>
<!--<![endif]-->

Simpler option: square corners in Outlook

If rounded corners in Outlook don't matter, put the background and padding on a table cell and the link inside it. Outlook shows a square button, other clients show your rounded one.

<table role="presentation" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" border="0">
  <tr>
    <td bgcolor="#1D44B8" style="border-radius:5px;padding:12px 24px;">
      <a href="https://example.com" style="color:#ffffff;font-family:Arial,sans-serif;font-size:16px;font-weight:bold;text-decoration:none;">Get started</a>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>

Frequently asked questions

Why does my button work in Gmail but not Outlook?

Gmail renders CSS like a browser; classic Outlook uses Word, which ignores border-radius and padding on links.

Is the whole VML button clickable?

Yes. The href on v:roundrect makes the entire shape a link in Outlook.

Do I need the VML namespace?

Yes. Without the xmlns attributes, Outlook ignores the VML. Stampcheck flags VML used without its namespace.

Can I use an image as a button instead?

You can, but it disappears when images are off, which is the default for many Outlook users. A real HTML button is safer.
